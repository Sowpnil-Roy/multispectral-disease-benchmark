# Code–paper audit

This file records how the paper and the code were checked against each other
before release, what was added to the code, and what was corrected in the
paper. The automatic check (`src/verify_paper.py`) covers every number that
appears in the text; `docs/CLAIMS_REPORT.md` lists them all with the computed
value.

## 1. Reproducibility

* The full pipeline was rerun from an empty `results/` directory: the
  simulated spectra were regenerated (all 16 arrays have the SHA-256 recorded
  in `data/checksums.json`) and every stage was executed again.
* `src/compare_results.py` found **no difference in any stored number** apart
  from latency timings, which depend on the machine.
* All five figures regenerated by `src/figs.py` were identical, pixel for
  pixel, to the figures in the paper.
* The rows produced by `src/tables.py` for Tables III, IV and V are identical,
  character for character, to the rows in the paper's LaTeX source.

## 5. Claims that depended on one noise realization

The band sets in the paper (Greedy-k, GreedyX-k) come from one sensor-noise
realization. `selstab` repeated the selection under two more.

**Silicon library.** The importance regions (740–770 nm and 960–990 nm) held
in both repeats. The greedy picks did not: the red-edge and water-shoulder
picks moved by 0–20 nm, but the visible and NIR picks moved by 10–140 nm (neither repeat
picked a yellow band near 590 nm). Five selected
bands still reached 0.73–0.75 macro-F1 (best model), against 0.51 for
RedEdge-MX-like.

**SWIR-extended library.** The first pick was 1450 nm in the reported run but
2000 nm and 1550 nm in the repeats, always a SWIR band beyond 1400 nm. The
alternative five-band SWIR sets still beat the alternative silicon sets
(0.79–0.82 vs. 0.73–0.75, best model) and kept a higher LR wilt recall
(0.74–0.78 vs. 0.46–0.64). Linear and shallow models again transferred better
than trees on domain B.

The paper text was changed accordingly: the old sentence "in a run with
another noise realization they moved by 10–20 nm" was replaced by the measured
shifts; the Abstract and contribution (iii) now say "a SWIR band (1450 nm in
the reported run)" rather than presenting 1450 nm as the single best
wavelength; Sec. IV-C reports the two alternative first picks and their
results; the Discussion recommends "one SWIR band beyond 1400 nm".

## 6. Other observations (no change needed)

* The vegetation-index baseline uses its own noise realization of the
  RedEdge-MX bands (tag `"vi"`), not the exact features of the RedEdge-MX row.
* The MLP stops at `max_iter=350` and raises a `ConvergenceWarning` for some
  configurations; all reported numbers use this fixed setting.
* `edge.json` contains `full59_f1A/B` from the 120-tree importance forest;
  these are not the 59-band numbers in the paper (those are in
  `full59.json`).
* Latency ratios in Sec. IV-F (≈50× and 125×) hold for the paper's machine.
  On another machine a fresh run gives different ratios; the ordering (LR and
  MLP fastest, RF and HGB ~20 µs, SVM slowest) is what transfers.
